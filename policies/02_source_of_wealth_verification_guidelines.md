# Source of Wealth Verification Guidelines
**Version:** 1.3  
**Effective Date:** 1 March 2025  
**Owner:** MLRO  
**Review Cycle:** Annual

---

## 1. Purpose

Source of Wealth (SoW) verification establishes how a client accumulated the assets they are bringing to or through the regulated firm. It is distinct from Source of Funds (SoF), which covers where the specific funds in a transaction originate. Both may be required; this document governs SoW.

Unverified or inadequately explained wealth is one of the most common findings in AML regulatory reviews. This policy sets minimum documentation standards for each recognised wealth category and defines when additional corroboration is required.

---

## 2. Recognised Wealth Categories

The platform recognises five primary Source of Wealth categories. Where a client's wealth derives from more than one category, each must be documented and verified independently.

| # | Category | Description |
|---|----------|-------------|
| 1 | Employment / Salary | Wealth accumulated through employment income over time |
| 2 | Business Ownership or Sale | Wealth from ownership of, or proceeds from the sale of, a business |
| 3 | Inheritance | Wealth received through a bequest, estate distribution or gift |
| 4 | Investment Portfolio | Wealth accumulated through returns on financial investments |
| 5 | Property / Real Estate | Wealth accumulated through property ownership, rental income or sale proceeds |

If a client's wealth does not fit any of these categories, the case must be escalated to the MLRO before the file progresses. The MLRO will determine whether the wealth source is sufficiently explained and documented and will record the decision.

---

## 3. Documentation Requirements by Category

### 3.1 Employment / Salary

Required documents:

- Payslips for the most recent **three consecutive months**, issued by the employer and showing gross and net pay
- A letter from the employer confirming the client's role, salary, and length of employment (on headed paper, signed by HR or a director)
- Bank statements for the most recent **three months**, showing salary credits matching the payslips

Where a client is self-employed, the following replace the employer letter and payslips:

- Most recent **two years** of filed tax returns or self-assessment summaries
- Accountant-certified profit and loss statements for the same period
- Bank statements for the most recent six months

A single payslip or a verbal confirmation of employment is not sufficient. All three documents must be present and consistent with each other.

### 3.2 Business Ownership or Sale

**For current business ownership:**

- Certificate of incorporation or equivalent
- Most recent two years of audited or accountant-certified financial statements
- Confirmation of the client's ownership percentage and role (director register, shareholder register, or equivalent)
- Bank statements for the business showing regular income activity

**For proceeds from a business sale:**

- Signed and dated sale and purchase agreement
- Confirmation from a solicitor or notary that the transaction completed, including the net proceeds figure
- Bank statements showing the sale proceeds being received into the client's account
- If the sale occurred more than five years ago, a statutory declaration or equivalent sworn statement explaining the intervening period

Where the client is the sole or majority owner of a cash-intensive business (e.g., retail, hospitality, money services), additional scrutiny applies and the case must be escalated for EDD regardless of the risk score.

### 3.3 Inheritance

Required documents:

- A copy of the **grant of probate** or equivalent legal authority establishing the estate distribution
- The **will** or a court-issued letter of administration, confirming the client's entitlement
- Bank statements or investment account statements showing receipt of the inherited funds or assets
- Where assets rather than cash were inherited (e.g., property, shares), an independent valuation dated at or near the time of inheritance

A minimum of **two independent sources** must corroborate the inheritance claim. A statement by the client alone is not sufficient. Where the estate originated in a high-risk jurisdiction, additional documentation confirming the source of the deceased's wealth may be required.

### 3.4 Investment Portfolio

Required documents:

- Statements from the investment account(s) covering the most recent **12 months**, issued by the regulated investment firm or custodian
- Confirmation of the original source of funds used to seed the investment portfolio (which may itself require documentation under another category)
- Evidence of the investment firm's regulated status

Where returns are unusually high relative to the stated investment strategy, the reviewing officer must request a written explanation and may require additional corroboration.

### 3.5 Property / Real Estate

**For rental income:**

- Tenancy agreements currently in force
- Bank statements showing regular rental credits for the most recent **six months**
- Evidence of property ownership (title deed, land registry entry or equivalent)

**For sale proceeds:**

- Completion statement from the conveyancer or notary
- Land registry entry confirming the transfer of ownership
- Bank statements showing receipt of the sale proceeds

Where the property is in a jurisdiction with limited land registry transparency, a lawyer's certificate of title or equivalent must be obtained.

---

## 4. Corroboration Standards

| Risk Band | Minimum Independent Sources |
|-----------|----------------------------|
| Low | 1 source (self-declared + one supporting document) |
| Medium | 2 sources (documents must be independently issued) |
| High / EDD | 2 sources minimum; MLRO may require more depending on complexity |

Documents issued by the client themselves (e.g., a self-prepared spreadsheet, an unsigned letter) do not count as independent sources. All sources must be issued by a third party (employer, solicitor, bank, accountant, government body).

---

## 5. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

The reviewing officer must escalate to the MLRO where any of the following are present:

- Wealth is claimed to have originated in a high-risk jurisdiction without independent corroboration
- Documents appear inconsistent with each other (e.g., salary credits do not match payslip amounts)
- The client is unable or unwilling to provide required documentation without a plausible explanation
- The scale of the wealth appears disproportionate to the stated occupation or business activity
- The client claims wealth from a category not listed in Section 2
- Documents show signs of alteration or are of unusually poor quality

Escalation does not automatically mean the relationship cannot proceed. It means the MLRO must review and document the decision.

---

## 6. Record Keeping

All Source of Wealth documents and the verification outcome must be stored on the client file. The file must record:

- The category or categories of wealth claimed
- Each document collected, including date, issuer, and document type
- The verification outcome (Verified / Incomplete / Escalated)
- The name of the reviewing officer and date of review

Records must be retained in accordance with the retention schedule in the CDD Policy (Policy 01).
